# Landingpage-Prompt v4 – Stil: Japandi

_Erzeugt aus der Vorlage „One-Page Landing Page Prompt“. Stil per Zufall gewählt (`secrets.choice`) → **Japandi**.
Statt eines erfundenen Geschäftskonzepts wird das reale Unternehmen verwendet: Munich Express – Kfz-Gutachter & Abschleppdienst Kokaj, München._

---

Design a single-page landing page in the Japandi style – the quiet meeting point of Japanese restraint and Scandinavian warmth – for a towing service and certified vehicle assessor in Munich, conceived around one idea: after an accident everything is loud, and this page is the first place where it becomes quiet again. Visitors arrive shaken, often on a phone at the roadside, and should feel their breathing slow the moment the page opens: wide pale surfaces, a single calm statement, a phone number that is impossible to miss but never shouts. As they scroll, the hierarchy should unfold like walking through a tidy, sunlit room – one thing at a time, generous space between each thought, nothing competing for attention. Let colour enter sparingly and with meaning: the warm tones of oiled oak and stone, a muted moss green for reassurance, and a single deep red used only like a hand-pressed seal of approval, so that every coloured element carries emotional weight.

The design philosophy is calm competence. Typography should feel composed and trustworthy – light to medium weights, airy letter-spacing, short lines that are easy to read under stress, with the occasional vertical label as a quiet nod to Japanese writing. Interactions and animations should feel like breath and ink: slow fades, elements settling gently into place, a brush-stroke circle that draws itself once, a red seal that presses softly onto the page when the certification appears – never snappy, never bouncing, always unhurried. The single scroll should move emotionally from relief (“someone is here, right now”) to orientation (“what happened to you, and what to do next”), to understanding (“these are your rights, this is how it works”), to trust (“these are the people who will help you”), and finally to a calm, confident call to action that feels like an open door rather than a sales pitch.

Draw inspiration from the feeling of a minimalist tea room at dawn, a Nordic workshop where every tool hangs in its place, hand-planed timber, washi paper letting soft light through, raked gravel, and the discipline of a master craftsman who explains rather than sells. Think of the reassurance of a well-run ryokan, where care is shown through order, attention and silence rather than decoration. These references should shape the page into something that feels handmade, precise and deeply human: restrained in quantity, rich in quality, with each section placed as deliberately as a single branch in a vase – one cohesive landing page whose calm is itself the promise of the service.
